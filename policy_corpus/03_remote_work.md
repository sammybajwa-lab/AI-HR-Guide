# MF-POL-201 — Flexible and Remote Work

**Owner:** People Operations and Workplace Operations  
**Effective:** 1 February 2026  
**Policy version:** 4.2

## 1. Work arrangement categories
Morrowfen uses three work-arrangement labels: **site-based**, **flexible**, and **remote**. The employee profile maintained by People Operations is the source of record for the assigned label.

A site-based role is expected on site for the workweek stated by the business unit. A flexible role may split time between an assigned office and an approved home workspace. A remote role has no routine office attendance requirement but still has an assigned employing location for payroll, tax, and legal purposes.

The label is not permission to work from any location. Temporary work outside the employee's approved work state or country is governed jointly by this policy and MF-POL-202.

## 2. Home-workspace expectations
Employees working remotely must have a private or appropriately controlled place to work, reliable internet suitable for their duties, and the ability to protect confidential information from household members and visitors. Public spaces can be used for low-sensitivity work only when privacy and security can be maintained. Restricted information must not be discussed where it can be overheard.

Company devices must be used for company work when a device has been issued. Personal devices may be used only for services specifically approved for bring-your-own-device access by Information Security.

## 3. Flexible attendance
For flexible roles, the business unit sets anchor days or attendance expectations. Managers may make reasonable short-term adjustments for project work, customer meetings, or personal circumstances. A long-term change to the assigned arrangement requires People Operations approval and an updated employee record.

## 4. Temporary domestic remote work
Working temporarily from another U.S. state is not treated as ordinary home working. An employee who expects to work outside the approved home state for **more than 10 business days in any rolling 90-day period** must request approval before the travel begins. People Operations and Tax Operations review the location, duration, employment status, and any local registration or payroll concerns.

Ten business days is a review threshold, not an automatic entitlement. A shorter stay can still require review if the employee will perform regulated work, handle location-restricted data, or work from a client site.

## 5. Temporary international remote work
International remote work requires **advance written approval from People Operations, Tax Operations, and Information Security** before any work is performed outside the employee's country of employment. Manager approval alone is not enough.

The standard temporary international remote-work allowance is **up to 20 working days in a rolling 12-month period**. Approval can be shorter than 20 days. A request above 20 working days is outside the standard allowance and must be handled as a potential formal assignment, transfer, or leave arrangement. The employee must not begin the arrangement while that review is pending.

Approval can be denied because of immigration, payroll, tax, sanctions, data-security, customer-contract, time-zone, or business-continuity constraints. Personal travel bookings do not create an exception.

## 6. Restricted locations and security
Information Security may maintain a list of countries or regions where company systems cannot be accessed. The list can change in response to legal or security conditions. Employees should not rely on an old approval for a new trip. Access from a restricted location may be blocked automatically.

For approved international work, Information Security may require a managed laptop, current endpoint protection, multi-factor authentication, VPN use, and limits on locally stored data. Device requirements are described in MF-POL-301 and MF-POL-402.

## 7. Working hours and availability
Remote work does not change the employee's normal work schedule unless the manager approves a schedule change. Teams may define collaboration hours. Employees crossing time zones remain responsible for agreed meetings and customer obligations, subject to local working-time rules.

## 8. Costs
Ordinary home internet and utilities are not reimbursed unless required by law or a location addendum. Approved equipment is handled under MF-POL-402. Travel chosen primarily so the employee can work from a personal destination is not business travel and is not reimbursable.

## 9. Safety and reporting
An employee should report a work-related injury that occurs while working remotely just as they would report an on-site injury. The company may ask for facts needed to assess the incident but will not inspect a private home without a lawful basis and appropriate notice.

## 10. Exceptions
An exception must be in writing from the function that owns the rule being changed. A manager can approve team coverage; a manager cannot waive tax, immigration, information-security, or legal requirements. If evidence is incomplete, the correct outcome is pending review, not assumed approval.

# MF-POL-401 — Business Expenses and Travel

**Owner:** Finance Operations  
**Effective:** 1 March 2026  
**Policy version:** 3.3

## 1. General standard
Morrowfen reimburses reasonable, necessary, and properly documented business expenses. Personal convenience does not turn a personal purchase into a business expense. Employees are expected to use judgment, choose cost-effective options, and obtain required approval before committing the company to unusual or high-cost items.

## 2. Receipts
An itemized receipt is required for any single expense of **$25 or more**. A card slip showing only the total is not itemized. If a receipt is genuinely unavailable, the employee may submit a missing-receipt statement; repeated missing receipts may be escalated to Finance Operations.

Expense reports should be submitted within **30 calendar days** of the transaction or the end of business travel. Reports more than 60 days late require manager and Finance Operations review.

## 3. Approval thresholds
Routine business expenses below $500 require the employee's manager approval. A single non-travel purchase from $500 through $2,499 requires manager approval plus the relevant budget owner. Purchases of $2,500 or more must use Procurement unless an emergency exception is documented.

Employees may not split a purchase into smaller transactions to avoid an approval threshold.

## 4. Air, rail, hotel, and ground travel
Business travel should be booked through the approved travel channel when available. Economy class is the default for flights under six scheduled hours. A different class requires a documented exception under the travel standard.

Reasonable hotel, public transit, taxi, rideshare, and rental-car costs are reimbursable when connected to approved business travel. Personal sightseeing, companion travel, minibar charges, entertainment without a business purpose, and traffic or parking fines are not reimbursable.

## 5. Meals
Meals while traveling overnight for business are reimbursable within the location guideline maintained by Finance. Alcohol is not reimbursed unless it is part of an approved customer or team event and the approving manager is permitted to authorize it. An employee's ordinary meal during a normal workday is not reimbursable merely because the employee worked from an office.

## 6. Home-office items
Home-office equipment is governed by MF-POL-402. An employee should not buy a chair, monitor, laptop, or other durable equipment and assume it will be reimbursed. The equipment policy states what is company-issued, what can be reimbursed, and what needs pre-approval.

## 7. Laptops and technology
Company laptops are provisioned through IT Asset Management. Employees should not purchase a laptop on a personal card unless IT and the budget owner approve the exception in writing before purchase. Software subscriptions should use the approved procurement route and must satisfy Information Security requirements.

## 8. Client-billable expenses
When an expense will be passed to a customer, the employee must also follow the client contract and project rules. The stricter rule applies. A cost may be allowable under company policy but not billable to a customer.

## 9. Personal extensions
If an employee extends business travel for personal reasons, the company reimburses only the business portion. Any increase in airfare, lodging, or ground transportation caused by the personal extension is the employee's responsibility. The employee should document a business-only comparison when needed.

## 10. Prohibited expenses
The company does not reimburse personal clothing, personal subscriptions, fines, penalties caused by employee conduct, cash-equivalent gift cards unless part of an approved program, unapproved upgrades, or expenses that violate law or company policy.

## 11. Disputes
Finance Operations may return an expense report for missing documentation or clarification. A returned report is not necessarily a denial. If policy evidence is unclear, the assistant should state what approval is needed rather than invent a reimbursement decision.
## 12. Example decisions
A $42 airport meal on approved overnight business travel can be reimbursable if it is within the applicable meal guideline and supported by an itemized receipt. A $320 home-office chair is not decided under this policy alone; MF-POL-402 controls and requires checking remaining allowance and the single-item pre-approval rule. A $1,200 software subscription requires the relevant budget owner and the approved software/procurement route. A personally purchased laptop should be declined unless the documented IT and budget exception existed before purchase.

## 13. Currency and taxes
Foreign-currency expenses should be submitted with the card statement conversion or the approved expense-system exchange rate. Employees should not add their own exchange-rate buffer. Tax treatment of a reimbursement is determined by Payroll and Finance, not by the employee or approving manager.

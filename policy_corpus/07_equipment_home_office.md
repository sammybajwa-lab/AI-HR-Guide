# MF-POL-402 — Equipment and Home Office

**Owner:** IT Asset Management and Workplace Operations  
**Effective:** 1 March 2026  
**Policy version:** 2.2

## 1. Standard equipment
Employees whose roles require computer access receive a company-managed laptop and required security accessories. Standard equipment remains company property. IT Asset Management records the asset, serial number, assigned employee, and return status.

A second laptop is not standard. A technical, testing, accessibility, or business-continuity need can be reviewed by IT and the employee's manager.

## 2. Home-office allowance
Regular remote employees and flexible employees approved to work from home at least two days per week are eligible for a **one-time home-office allowance of up to $350** after their arrangement is recorded in the HR system. Eligible uses include a monitor, keyboard, mouse, basic desk, task lighting, and ergonomic accessories.

A chair may be purchased under the allowance if it fits within the remaining balance. Any single home-office item costing more than $250 requires manager pre-approval. The allowance cannot be used for home decor, televisions, household appliances, construction, rent, or recurring utilities.

## 3. Monitors and peripherals
IT may issue standard monitors and peripherals directly instead of reimbursing a personal purchase. Employees should check the equipment catalog before buying. Company-issued equipment does not reduce a statutory reimbursement obligation where local law requires separate treatment.

## 4. Internet and mobile service
Ordinary residential internet is not reimbursed under the company-wide plan unless a location addendum or role-specific agreement says otherwise. Approved business mobile plans or stipends are handled through the telecom program. International roaming incurred during personal travel is not reimbursable merely because the employee performed voluntary remote work.

## 5. Repairs and replacement
Employees should report damaged or malfunctioning company equipment to IT. Normal wear is repaired or replaced by the company. Damage caused by theft should also be reported under MF-POL-301. Employees should not arrange a third-party repair of a managed laptop without IT authorization.

## 6. Personal equipment
Personal equipment used for work remains the employee's property. Reimbursement does not automatically transfer ownership unless the approval states otherwise. Company data stored on a personal device remains subject to security rules and may need to be removed when employment ends.

## 7. Procurement and security
Technology that connects to company systems must meet Information Security requirements. A product being sold by a reputable retailer is not evidence that it is approved. Software, network equipment, storage devices, and smart peripherals can require security review.

## 8. Return of company property
Employees must return company property when requested, when changing roles if the equipment is no longer needed, and at separation. The standard return window is seven calendar days after the employee receives shipping instructions, unless another date is agreed in writing.

## 9. Accessibility equipment
An item requested because of a disability, medical restriction, or accessibility need should not be evaluated solely under the $350 allowance. The employee should be referred to the accommodation process under MF-POL-702. Approved accommodation equipment can exceed normal home-office limits.

## 10. Expense examples
A $180 monitor for an eligible flexible employee can normally be reimbursed from an unused allowance after manager approval and receipt submission. A $900 ergonomic chair is outside the standard allowance and should not be approved through ordinary expense processing; it may be considered through an accommodation or separately documented business exception. A personally purchased laptop is not standard reimbursable equipment without prior IT and budget approval.
## 11. Ordering sequence
Employees should first check whether the item is available through the standard catalog. If it is not, they should confirm eligibility, remaining allowance, and any required pre-approval before purchase. After purchase, the employee submits the itemized receipt through the expense process. IT or Workplace Operations may ask for an asset record for equipment that remains company property.

## 12. Examples of non-standard requests
A specialized monitor needed for color-critical work can be reviewed as a role requirement even if it exceeds the ordinary allowance. A sit-stand desk requested as a preference is considered against the ordinary allowance and approval limits. The same desk requested as part of an accommodation should be routed under MF-POL-702 instead of being denied because of the ordinary limit.

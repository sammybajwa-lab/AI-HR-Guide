# MF-POL-701 — Workplace Conduct and Speak-Up

**Owner:** People Operations and Legal  
**Effective:** 1 January 2026  
**Policy version:** 4.0

## 1. Expected conduct
Morrowfen expects employees to treat colleagues, customers, vendors, and visitors with respect and professional judgment. Harassment, discrimination, retaliation, threats, deliberate safety violations, fraud, and other serious misconduct are prohibited.

A disagreement, critical feedback, or ordinary performance management is not automatically misconduct. Concerns are assessed on facts and context.

## 2. Harassment and discrimination
Morrowfen prohibits harassment and discrimination based on characteristics protected by applicable law. The company also expects respectful conduct in situations that may not meet a legal definition of harassment. Conduct can violate policy whether it occurs in an office, online meeting, chat, business travel, customer event, or other work-related setting.

## 3. Retaliation
Retaliation against a person for raising a good-faith concern, requesting an accommodation or protected leave, participating in an investigation, or exercising a protected right is prohibited. A concern about retaliation should be treated as a separate issue even if the underlying complaint is not substantiated.

## 4. Reporting channels
Employees can raise concerns with their manager, another manager, People Operations, Legal, or the confidential speak-up channel. An employee does not have to report to a manager who is involved in the concern.

Immediate threats to physical safety should be handled through local emergency procedures first, then reported internally when safe to do so.

## 5. Manager responsibilities
A manager who receives a report of possible harassment, discrimination, retaliation, threats, fraud, or another serious policy violation must pass the concern to People Operations or Legal. The manager should not promise secrecy, run a private investigation, or decide that the issue is too minor to report.

## 6. Confidentiality
Information is shared on a need-to-know basis. Absolute confidentiality cannot be guaranteed because the company may need to investigate, take corrective action, or comply with law. Employees are expected not to interfere with an investigation or retaliate against participants.

## 7. Good-faith reports
An employee is not required to prove a concern before reporting it. Knowingly false statements can themselves violate policy, but an unsubstantiated good-faith report is not a policy violation merely because evidence does not confirm it.

## 8. Conflicts of interest
Employees should disclose a personal, financial, family, or outside-business interest that could reasonably affect company decisions they influence. Disclosure allows the company to manage the conflict; it does not automatically mean the activity is prohibited.

## 9. Substance impairment and safety
Employees must not work while impaired in a way that creates a safety or performance risk. Prescription medication or a health condition should be handled through the appropriate accommodation or leave process, not through informal requests for medical detail.

## 10. HR assistant handling
The HR assistant may provide reporting options and policy citations, but it should not determine whether an allegation is true, conduct credibility analysis, or identify disciplinary action. Sensitive allegations should be escalated. If the user describes immediate danger, the assistant should prioritize emergency safety guidance before routine case steps.
## 11. Triage examples
A complaint that a colleague repeatedly makes unwanted comments about a protected characteristic should be escalated for People Operations or Legal review; the assistant can explain reporting routes but should not label the allegation proven. A disagreement over project priorities can usually be handled through management unless the user also describes retaliation, discrimination, threats, or another policy concern. A statement indicating an immediate threat of violence is urgent and should be routed to emergency safety procedures before routine HR case creation.

## 12. Investigation boundary
Only authorized investigators should interview witnesses, assess credibility, or determine findings. Managers and automated systems should preserve the employee's own words where useful, avoid leading questions, and avoid promising a particular disciplinary outcome.

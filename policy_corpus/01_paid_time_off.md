# MF-POL-101 — Paid Time Off (PTO)

**Owner:** People Operations  
**Effective:** 1 January 2026  
**Applies to:** U.S.-based regular employees unless a local addendum says otherwise  
**Policy version:** 3.1

## 1. Purpose and scope
Morrowfen Systems uses one PTO bank for vacation, personal time, and short non-medical absences. Sick time required by state or local law is tracked separately where applicable. This document governs the company PTO bank; statutory sick leave rules can be more generous and take precedence when required.

PTO is intended to be used. Managers should plan coverage rather than discourage reasonable time away. At the same time, an approved absence is not automatic merely because an employee has a balance. Coverage, timing, notice, and applicable blackout periods still matter.

## 2. Eligibility and accrual
Regular full-time employees accrue PTO each pay period beginning on their first day of employment. Regular part-time employees scheduled for at least 20 hours per week accrue on a prorated basis. Interns, temporary agency workers, and independent contractors do not accrue company PTO unless a written engagement letter states otherwise.

Annualized full-time accrual is based on completed service at the start of the calendar year:

- Less than 3 years: 15 days / 120 hours per year.
- 3 through 5 years: 20 days / 160 hours per year.
- More than 5 years: 25 days / 200 hours per year.

PTO accrues each pay period and is not front-loaded. Employees may use only accrued, available hours unless People Operations approves a documented exception. The normal carryover limit is 40 hours into the next calendar year. A location-specific law may require a different treatment; People Operations maintains those addenda.

## 3. Request timing
Requests for one or two consecutive workdays should normally be submitted at least 2 calendar days before the first day off. Requests for three to five consecutive workdays should normally be submitted at least 5 calendar days in advance. Requests for more than five consecutive workdays should normally be submitted at least 15 calendar days in advance.

These are minimum planning windows, not guarantees of approval. Employees should submit foreseeable time off as early as practical. An unexpected emergency can be reported as soon as the employee is able; the advance-notice rule does not apply to an event that could not reasonably be anticipated.

## 4. Approval and coverage
The employee's manager approves or declines PTO after considering available balance, team coverage, customer or operational commitments, and overlapping requests. Managers may ask for a coverage note or handoff plan for absences of three or more workdays. They should not ask for private medical details when a medical or protected leave process may apply.

If multiple employees request the same dates and the team cannot support all requests, the manager should use a consistent method such as request order, rotating priority, or material business need. Protected leave is handled separately and must not be treated as ordinary discretionary PTO.

## 5. Blackout periods
A business unit may publish a temporary blackout period for a launch, financial close, scheduled migration, inventory event, or other critical operation. A blackout must be communicated in writing before requests are denied on that basis. People Operations review is required for a blackout longer than 10 business days.

Previously approved PTO should not be cancelled except for a serious business continuity issue. If a cancellation causes a non-refundable personal cost, the employee may ask People Operations to review reimbursement on a case-by-case basis; reimbursement is not automatic.

## 6. Using PTO with holidays and weekends
Company holidays do not reduce the PTO bank. If a holiday falls inside an approved PTO period, only scheduled working hours on non-holiday days are deducted. Weekend days are not deducted unless they are part of the employee's normal work schedule.

PTO is recorded in hours. For a standard eight-hour schedule, three PTO days equals 24 hours. Employees with alternative schedules use the number of hours they were scheduled to work on each missed day.

## 7. Negative balances and unpaid time
Managers cannot approve a negative PTO balance on their own. A short negative balance may be approved by People Operations in writing for a documented exceptional circumstance. Unpaid personal time is not a substitute for PTO and also requires People Operations review unless required by law or an approved leave plan.

## 8. Separation from employment
Unused PTO is paid at separation only where required by applicable law or a written employment agreement. The payroll team applies the rule for the employee's work location. Employees should not make assumptions about payout based on another employee's state or prior employer.

## 9. Questions and escalation
Balance disputes go first to Payroll Operations. Questions about protected leave, statutory sick time, or an exception to this policy go to People Operations. When policy evidence is incomplete or a location-specific rule may apply, the HR assistant should state that limitation rather than infer a result.

# MF-POL-202 — Temporary Work Location, Tax, and Immigration Review

**Owner:** People Operations and Tax Operations  
**Effective:** 1 February 2026  
**Policy version:** 2.4

## 1. Why location review exists
Where work is physically performed can create payroll withholding, employer registration, tax, immigration, labor-law, insurance, and customer-contract obligations. The employee's approved work location is therefore a controlled employment record, not merely a mailing address.

This policy explains when a location review is required. It does not provide personal tax or immigration advice. Employees remain responsible for personal travel documents and should obtain their own professional advice when needed.

## 2. Change of residence
An employee who plans to move their primary residence to another state or country must tell People Operations before the move. A mailing-address change in the HR system does not itself authorize a new work location. People Operations will confirm whether the role can be employed from the proposed location and whether payroll can support it.

## 3. Domestic temporary location review
A planned period of work in another U.S. state that exceeds 10 business days in a rolling 90-day period requires a pre-travel review under MF-POL-201. The review considers the destination, expected workdays, employee's employing entity, role, customer obligations, and prior days worked in that location.

Tax Operations may set a lower threshold for a particular jurisdiction. When that happens, the location-specific instruction controls. Employees must accurately report expected days; deliberately splitting a stay into separate requests does not avoid the rolling-period test.

## 4. International travel versus international work
Being physically outside the country while on vacation is not international remote work if no work is performed. Checking an emergency message is not intended to convert an ordinary vacation into a work arrangement, but employees should avoid routine work unless approval exists.

Any planned productive work from another country requires approval before the first work session. This includes customer calls, coding, document drafting, analysis, system administration, or other normal job duties.

## 5. Standard international threshold
The standard temporary international work limit is 20 working days in a rolling 12-month period, subject to all required approvals. Requests above that threshold are routed for formal assignment or transfer review. The 20-day threshold does not override a more restrictive immigration, tax, sanctions, or customer rule.

A six-week request will normally exceed the standard allowance if it includes more than 20 working days. The appropriate result is **not eligible under the standard temporary arrangement; formal review required**. The assistant should not convert calendar weeks into working days without the requested schedule if that detail matters.

## 6. Immigration status
Employees must have the legal right to perform work in the destination. Tourist or visitor status may not permit work. Morrowfen does not instruct employees to work in a country where authorization is uncertain. Immigration questions are escalated to approved counsel through People Operations.

## 7. Tax and payroll
Tax Operations determines whether temporary work creates withholding, payroll reporting, registration, or employer tax obligations. The employee should not promise a customer or manager that a location is acceptable before the review is complete.

## 8. Sanctions and trade restrictions
A destination may be prohibited or limited because of sanctions, export-control, or customer restrictions. Information Security and Legal may block system access or prohibit work. These controls take priority over business convenience.

## 9. Required request details
A complete location request includes employee ID, destination city and country or U.S. state, proposed start and end dates, expected working days, reason for travel, whether customer systems or restricted data will be accessed, and a manager acknowledgment of coverage.

Missing employee ID, destination, or approximate duration is a material gap. The assistant should ask a clarifying question instead of assuming values.

## 10. Decisions and records
Approval must be documented in the HR case record or approved workflow. Verbal approval is insufficient for international work. The final record should state the location, date range, conditions, and approving functions.

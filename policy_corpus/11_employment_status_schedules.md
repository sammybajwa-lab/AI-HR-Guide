# MF-POL-602 — Employment Status and Work Schedules

**Owner:** People Operations  
**Effective:** 1 January 2026  
**Policy version:** 2.1

## 1. Employment categories
Morrowfen records employees as regular full-time, regular part-time, intern, or temporary. Independent contractors and agency workers are not employees in the HR system used for this policy. Classification affects benefits, PTO, scheduling, and other programs, but local law can impose additional rules.

## 2. Full-time and part-time schedules
For company program purposes, regular full-time generally means a standard schedule of 40 hours per week. Regular part-time means a regular schedule below 40 hours. Benefits eligibility uses the separate threshold stated in MF-POL-501, not the full-time label alone.

## 3. Standard workweek
The standard administrative workweek runs Monday through Sunday. Most U.S. office employees work Monday through Friday, but customer and operational teams may use different schedules. Managers are responsible for publishing schedule expectations.

## 4. Alternative schedules
A compressed schedule, shifted schedule, or other recurring arrangement requires manager approval and may require People Operations review. An alternative schedule must still meet coverage, timekeeping, meal/rest, and overtime requirements that apply to the employee.

## 5. Timekeeping
Non-exempt employees must record all time worked and must not perform off-the-clock work. A manager must not ask an employee to omit time because it was unapproved. Unapproved overtime can be addressed as a performance or scheduling issue, but worked time must still be reported and paid as required.

## 6. Overtime
Non-exempt employees should obtain advance manager approval before working overtime when required by their team. The approval rule does not remove the obligation to record time actually worked. Exempt status and overtime eligibility are determined by People Operations and Payroll, not by job title alone.

## 7. Meal and rest periods
Employees must follow the meal and rest rules that apply to their work location. Because requirements vary, this company-wide policy does not state a single national schedule. The relevant local addendum or Payroll Operations guidance controls.

## 8. Schedule changes
Managers should give reasonable advance notice of recurring schedule changes. A permanent change in scheduled weekly hours must be sent to People Operations because it can affect pay, benefits, PTO accrual, and other records.

## 9. Attendance concerns
Repeated unplanned absence or lateness can be addressed under team attendance expectations, but managers should first consider whether the situation may involve protected leave, accommodation, or another legally protected reason. Employees should not be asked to disclose diagnoses to their manager.

## 10. Data source
For agentic workflows, the synthetic employee profile is the source for employment type, scheduled hours, manager, office, and work arrangement. If the profile is missing or stale, the assistant should request verification instead of filling the gap from policy text.
## 11. Classification examples
A regular part-time employee scheduled for 32 hours remains part-time for the employment-status field but can meet the 30-hour benefits threshold in MF-POL-501. An intern scheduled for 40 hours is not automatically converted to regular full-time. A remote work label also does not change employment status. Different fields answer different questions and should not be collapsed into one inferred classification.

## 12. Record corrections
If an employee says the schedule or classification in the profile is wrong, the assistant should explain the policy using the stored record but flag the discrepancy for People Operations. It must not overwrite the synthetic source record based on a chat statement.

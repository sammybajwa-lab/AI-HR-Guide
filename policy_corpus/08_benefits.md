# MF-POL-501 — Health and Welfare Benefits

**Owner:** Benefits Operations  
**Effective:** 1 January 2026  
**Policy version:** 3.0

## 1. Eligible employees
Regular employees scheduled for **30 or more hours per week** are eligible for the core U.S. health and welfare plan, subject to plan documents. Regular employees scheduled below 30 hours, interns, temporary agency workers, and independent contractors are not eligible for the company medical plan unless a plan document or law provides otherwise.

The formal plan documents control if this summary conflicts with them.

## 2. Coverage effective date
For a newly eligible employee, medical, dental, and vision coverage begins on the **first day of the month following 30 days of active employment**. If the 30th day falls on the first day of a month, coverage still begins on the first day of the following month.

The enrollment window is 30 calendar days from the employee's eligibility event. Failure to enroll during the window generally means waiting until annual enrollment unless the employee has a qualifying life event.

## 3. Dependents
Eligible dependents generally include a legal spouse, eligible domestic partner where permitted under the plan, and eligible children through the limiting age specified in the plan document. Verification documents may be required. The assistant should not determine dependent eligibility from relationship labels alone when plan-specific evidence is missing.

## 4. Qualifying life events
Marriage, divorce, birth or adoption, loss or gain of other coverage, and certain other events can permit a midyear election change. The employee must notify Benefits Operations within the plan's required window, generally 30 days unless the governing plan provides a different period.

## 5. Retirement plan
Eligible employees may participate in the company retirement plan according to the retirement plan document. Contribution limits, match formulas, and vesting can change and are maintained in the plan material rather than hard-coded into this summary. The HR assistant should retrieve the current plan source before quoting those figures.

## 6. Health savings and flexible spending accounts
Eligibility for an HSA depends on enrollment in an HSA-qualified medical plan and applicable tax rules. FSA participation depends on annual election rules. Benefits Operations can explain plan administration but does not provide individual tax advice.

## 7. Changes in work schedule
A reduction or increase in scheduled hours can affect eligibility. The employee profile and benefits record must be checked together. A manager's expectation about future hours is not a benefits eligibility determination.

## 8. Leave and benefits
Benefits may continue during certain approved leaves, subject to plan terms and required employee contributions. An employee going on leave should receive a written benefits continuation notice when applicable. The assistant should not promise uninterrupted coverage without checking the leave type and benefits status.

## 9. Termination of coverage
Coverage end dates at separation depend on the plan and event timing. Continuation rights, if applicable, are communicated separately. The assistant should direct a separated employee to the formal benefits notice rather than infer an exact end date without evidence.

## 10. Privacy
Medical claims and detailed health information are not stored in the general employee profile used by this project. The mock benefits tool returns only synthetic election and eligibility status. Sensitive medical questions should be escalated to Benefits Operations or the appropriate plan administrator.
## 11. Example eligibility checks
A regular employee scheduled for 40 hours per week meets the company hours threshold for core health-plan eligibility, subject to the plan's other terms and the waiting period. A regular employee scheduled for 24 hours does not meet the company-wide 30-hour threshold based on schedule alone. If an employee's profile shows 32 hours but the benefits record shows "pending review," the assistant should report the conflict and route it to Benefits Operations rather than choose one record.

## 12. Source-of-record rule
The benefits-status dataset answers whether a synthetic employee is enrolled, waived, pending, or not eligible. The policy explains eligibility rules. Neither source should be used to invent claim history, medical conditions, beneficiary choices, or other data not present in the project.

# MF-POL-702 — Workplace Accommodations and Accessibility

**Owner:** People Operations  
**Effective:** 1 January 2026  
**Policy version:** 2.5

## 1. Requesting an accommodation
An employee may request a workplace accommodation because of disability, medical restriction, pregnancy-related limitation, religion, or another protected need recognized by applicable law. The request does not need to use a specific phrase or form to start the process.

A manager who hears that a work rule, schedule, equipment setup, or workplace feature is difficult because of a protected need should connect the employee with People Operations rather than trying to diagnose the issue.

## 2. Interactive review
People Operations coordinates an individualized review of the employee's limitation, job duties, workplace conditions, and potential adjustments. The company may request documentation where permitted and necessary. Documentation should focus on functional limitations and accommodation needs rather than unnecessary medical detail.

## 3. Possible accommodations
Depending on the facts, an accommodation may involve schedule changes, leave, modified equipment, assistive technology, workspace changes, communication support, remote-work adjustments, or changes in how a non-essential task is performed. No single accommodation is guaranteed simply because another employee received it.

## 4. Equipment and cost limits
The ordinary home-office allowance in MF-POL-402 does not cap an approved accessibility accommodation. Employees should not be told to pay the difference personally because an accommodation item exceeds a standard office allowance. People Operations and the relevant equipment owner determine the approved solution.

## 5. Religious accommodations
Requests related to religious observance, dress, grooming, or practice are reviewed through the same People Operations channel. Managers should not ask employees to prove that a belief is familiar, mainstream, or shared by others.

## 6. Pregnancy-related adjustments
Employees can request adjustments related to pregnancy, childbirth, or related medical conditions. Depending on location and need, adjustments may include seating, breaks, schedule changes, temporary duty modification, or leave. People Operations determines the applicable process.

## 7. Confidentiality
Accommodation information is kept separate from ordinary performance records and shared only with people who need information to implement the approved arrangement. A manager may need to know the adjustment but usually does not need the underlying diagnosis.

## 8. Interim measures
When a review cannot be completed immediately, People Operations may approve a temporary measure when practical and safe. An interim measure is not a final determination and can be adjusted as more information becomes available.

## 9. Non-retaliation
Retaliation for requesting an accommodation, asking about rights, or participating in an accommodation review is prohibited. Concerns about retaliation should be escalated under MF-POL-701.

## 10. Assistant limits
The HR assistant can explain the process, identify relevant policy, and create a mock case summary with explicit confirmation. It should not evaluate medical evidence, decide whether a person has a disability, or state that a specific accommodation is legally required when the necessary facts are not available.
## 11. Process examples
An employee who says a standard chair worsens a medical restriction should be offered the accommodation route rather than simply told that the home-office allowance is exhausted. An employee requesting schedule flexibility for a religious observance can be referred to People Operations without being asked to explain theology. An employee who wants a preferred monitor with no stated protected need can use the normal equipment process. The routing depends on the reason described, not just the object being requested.

## 12. Implementation and review
An approved accommodation should identify what is changing, who needs to implement it, and when it should be reviewed if the need is temporary. Managers should raise implementation problems promptly rather than quietly discontinuing an approved adjustment.

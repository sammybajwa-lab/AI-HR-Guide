# MF-POL-601 — Onboarding, Identity, and Access

**Owner:** People Operations and IT  
**Effective:** 1 April 2026  
**Policy version:** 1.9

## 1. Pre-start requirements
Before a new hire starts, Recruiting and People Operations must have an accepted offer, completed right-to-work steps required for the work location, a confirmed start date, manager, job code, employment type, and approved work location. IT needs the same core data to provision identity and equipment.

The manager should submit any non-standard software or access request before the start date when possible. Access that is not required for the role should not be requested simply to avoid future effort.

## 2. First-day identity verification
Employees must complete required identity and employment verification steps through the approved process. Copies of identity documents should not be sent through ordinary email or team chat unless the approved process specifically requires it.

## 3. Account creation
IT creates the primary company account from the HR system record. The account name, manager, department, and employment status should match the source record. Shared accounts are prohibited unless Information Security approves a documented technical need.

## 4. Baseline access
Baseline access can include email, collaboration tools, HR systems, security training, and department resources. Sensitive applications require role-specific approval. A manager's approval is necessary but may not be sufficient for systems owned by Finance, Security, Legal, or a customer.

## 5. Security training
New employees must complete required security and privacy training within the assigned deadline, normally 10 business days from start. Certain system access can be withheld until required training is complete.

## 6. Equipment shipment
Remote employees should receive standard equipment through IT Asset Management. If shipment is delayed, the manager should coordinate a safe interim plan. A personal laptop should not be used for Restricted data merely because company equipment has not arrived.

## 7. Thirty-day onboarding check
Managers should complete a 30-day check covering role clarity, access gaps, required training, team norms, and any accommodation or equipment issues. The check is a management practice, not a performance rating.

## 8. Transfers and role changes
A transfer triggers an access review. Access needed in the old role should be removed when no longer required, while new access follows the normal approval route. Role changes that alter work location, employment status, or scheduled hours must also be updated in the HR record.

## 9. Separation
People Operations provides the final work date and separation type to IT. Access is disabled according to the separation plan, with immediate action for certain involuntary or security-related cases. Company equipment must be returned under MF-POL-402.

## 10. Onboarding checklist output
An HR assistant may create a checklist using these requirements and the employee profile. It should distinguish completed items from unknown items. If the structured data does not contain a completion status, the assistant should label the item "status not available" rather than assume completion.
## 11. Example checklist for a remote regular employee
Before start: verify accepted offer, employment type, work location, manager, start date, shipping address through the approved process, and required role access. First day: complete identity steps, activate the company account, enroll in multi-factor authentication, and confirm equipment receipt. First 10 business days: finish assigned security and privacy training. First 30 days: manager checks role clarity, access gaps, team norms, and any equipment or accommodation needs.

The checklist should show "not recorded" for a step that is absent from mock data. It should not mark an item complete merely because the employee has already started.

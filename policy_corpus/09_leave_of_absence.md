# MF-POL-502 — Leave of Absence

**Owner:** People Operations  
**Effective:** 1 January 2026  
**Policy version:** 2.7

## 1. Scope
This policy provides a company framework for medical, family, parental, military, bereavement, and personal leaves. Legal entitlement depends on the employee's location, service, hours, reason, and other facts. This summary does not replace federal, state, local, or plan documents.

## 2. When to contact People Operations
An employee should contact People Operations when an absence may last more than five consecutive scheduled workdays, is related to a serious health condition, involves care for a family member, relates to pregnancy or birth, involves military duty, or may require a workplace accommodation.

Managers should not diagnose the situation or ask for detailed medical information. Their role is to ensure immediate coverage and connect the employee with the leave process.

## 3. Medical and family leave
Eligibility for legally protected leave is determined by People Operations using the employee's work location, length of service, hours worked, employer coverage, and qualifying reason. The HR assistant may identify that a request appears to need leave review, but it should not declare legal eligibility without the required facts and policy evidence.

## 4. Parental leave
Eligible regular employees may have company-paid parental leave in addition to statutory benefits. The current duration and coordination rules are maintained in the parental leave plan document. Because those terms can change, the assistant should retrieve the current plan source before quoting a duration.

## 5. Bereavement
Regular employees may take up to five paid workdays for the death of an immediate family member and up to two paid workdays for another close relationship. A manager may approve additional PTO. People Operations can consider an exception when cultural, travel, or family circumstances require more time.

## 6. Personal leave
A discretionary unpaid personal leave may be considered for circumstances not covered by another leave program. Approval is not guaranteed. People Operations reviews business feasibility, length, prior leave, benefits impact, and applicable law. PTO should normally be used first unless the approved plan says otherwise.

## 7. Notice and documentation
Employees should provide as much notice as practical. Documentation requests must be limited to what is permitted and needed to administer the leave. Medical documentation is handled through the designated leave process, not ordinary team email or chat.

## 8. Return to work
People Operations coordinates return-to-work requirements when a leave or medical restriction requires them. A manager should not independently demand a medical release. An employee returning with restrictions may also need an accommodation review.

## 9. Non-retaliation
Employees will not be retaliated against for requesting or taking protected leave, raising a concern about leave rights, or participating in a leave investigation. Suspected retaliation should be escalated under MF-POL-701 and MF-POL-703.

## 10. Interaction with PTO
PTO may run concurrently with certain leaves when allowed by law and plan terms. The existence of a PTO balance does not determine whether an absence is protected. Conversely, an approved leave does not always mean every day is company-paid. People Operations provides the written designation and pay coordination.
## 11. Manager workflow
When an employee says an absence may involve a serious health condition or more than five scheduled workdays, the manager should record only the operational facts needed for coverage and refer the employee to People Operations. The manager should not ask for a diagnosis, medication list, or medical records. People Operations determines what documentation is permitted and necessary.

## 12. Examples
A planned three-day vacation is normally handled under PTO rather than leave. A two-week recovery from surgery should be routed for leave review even if the employee has enough PTO to cover the time. A request to change hours for an ongoing medical restriction may involve both leave and accommodation; the assistant should identify both routes instead of forcing the request into a single category.
